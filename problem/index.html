The Problem

Pre-eclampsia is a leading, and largely preventable, killer of mothers and babies.

Pre-eclampsia — a pregnancy complication marked by dangerously high blood pressure — is one of the leading causes of maternal and newborn death worldwide, and its burden falls hardest on women with the least access to consistent antenatal care.

Existing Solution

62%

reduction in preterm pre-eclampsia

Rolnik 2017 (ASPRE)

Early identification works. In the landmark ASPRE trial, screening pregnant women for pre-eclampsia risk and starting low-dose aspirin for those flagged high-risk cut preterm pre-eclampsia by more than half. The clinical solution already exists — what's missing in many settings is a practical, low-cost way to identify who is at risk early enough to act.

The Gap

28%

of women in Uganda skip antenatal care

Bhutada 2024

Early screening works — when it happens. But for the roughly 3 in 10 rural Ugandan women who don't complete the recommended course of antenatal visits, the warning signs of pre-eclampsia often go undetected until the condition is severe. PrevaSense is building the tools to close that gap, so that risk monitoring doesn't stop at the clinic door.

“It is a big gap we have, not knowing who to monitor over time, and being reluctant to send away patients without at-home monitoring.”

— Dr. Annettee Nakimuli
